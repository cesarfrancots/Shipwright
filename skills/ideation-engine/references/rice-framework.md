# RICE Framework

Use RICE to rank ideas consistently.

## Components

- Reach: how many users, accounts, or events will feel the change in a defined period
- Impact: how strongly the change improves the target outcome
- Confidence: how much evidence supports the estimates
- Effort: relative implementation cost, usually in person-weeks or team-weeks

## Simple Scoring Model

Use this formula:

`RICE = (Reach x Impact x Confidence) / Effort`

When the user has no baselines, use directional scales:

- Reach: 1 to 5
- Impact: 0.5, 1, 2, or 3
- Confidence: 0.5 to 1.0
- Effort: 1, 2, 3, 5, or 8

## Worked Example

Use a worked example to calibrate precision before scoring the full list.

| Idea | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort | RICE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger contextual nudges after three failed checkout attempts | 4 | 3 | 0.75 | 2 | 4.5 |
| Add a generic reminder email one hour later | 5 | 1 | 0.80 | 1 | 4.0 |
| Build a full recovery concierge flow | 3 | 3 | 0.60 | 5 | 1.1 |

Calibration notes:

- Use one decimal place for the final score when working from directional inputs.
- If two ideas differ only slightly, prefer relative ranking over false precision.
- Treat the worked example as a pattern for estimate quality, not as a required baseline.

## Interpretation

- High RICE with low confidence is a candidate for validation, not immediate commitment.
- Low effort ideas with medium impact often make strong near-term bets.
- High effort ideas require strategic justification, not just a numeric score.
- When two ideas are tied or near-tied, break the tie by higher confidence, then lower effort, then directness to the stated problem.
